# Interactions

An interaction earns its place only if the learner has to decide or infer something, gets feedback on that decision, and comes away understanding something they could not get from reading. Each one below is documented in the same six parts. All of them work without JavaScript as readable content (see [ARCHITECTURE.md](ARCHITECTURE.md#progressive-enhancement)), and all keep the learner's work only on their device.

---

## 1. Cost classifier

`costs-classifier-atelier`, on the costs and revenues brief and the Finance Desk.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **The learner does** | Classifies ten of Atelier Barro's costs twice: fixed, variable or semi-variable, and direct or indirect to one mug |
| **Must understand** | Behaviour (how a cost changes with output) and traceability (whether it can be traced to one unit) are separate questions |
| **Decides or infers** | For each cost, from its description (paid per mug, per month, as a share of the price, part fixed and part variable) |
| **Feedback** | Per cost: Right, One of two right, or Not yet, with the chosen and the correct option marked in words, and the reason; at the end, every cost sorted into its column |
| **Misconception diagnosed** | Labour is always fixed (the piece rate is variable); electricity is always variable (it has a standing charge); a cost the owner chooses, such as marketing, cannot be fixed; the owner's own salary is not a cost |
| **Objective** | Classify costs correctly before any calculation, because the classification decides where each cost goes in every formula |

## 2. Break-even workspace

`costs-break-even-atelier`, on the Finance Desk and the costs brief.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **The learner does** | Changes price, variable cost, the share of price paid in fees, fixed costs, forecast sales, target profit and capacity, or applies one of four scenarios from the case |
| **Must understand** | Contribution per unit links price and cost to break-even, the margin of safety and profit; a cost that is a share of the price rises when the price does |
| **Decides or infers** | Which scenario improves the margin of safety, and why a price rise with lower sales can still raise profit |
| **Feedback** | Figures, chart, data table and the eight-part working recalculate together; a plain-language reading and implication (thin, moderate or comfortable cushion, capacity warnings); invalid inputs are named beside the field |
| **Misconception diagnosed** | Rounding break-even down; ignoring price-linked costs; reading break-even as the goal rather than the floor; forgetting capacity |
| **Objective** | Calculate and interpret break-even, the margin of safety and target output, and state what the model assumes |

## 3. Find the error

`costs-error-diagnosis`, on the Finance Desk.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **The learner does** | Reads five worked solutions, each with one wrong line, and chooses the line |
| **Must understand** | The method, well enough to spot where a plausible answer goes wrong |
| **Decides or infers** | Which line breaks the reasoning |
| **Feedback** | Right line or Not this line, the correct line marked, why it is wrong and the corrected working |
| **Misconception diagnosed** | Rounding break-even down; dividing by price instead of contribution; keeping a price-linked fee at the old price; giving the margin of safety in money; subtracting the target profit |
| **Objective** | Check a calculation's method, not only its answer |

## 4. Special order panel (HL)

`costs-special-order-atelier`, on the Finance Desk contribution desk and the costs brief.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **The learner does** | Sets the order's price, size and extra costs, regular sales and capacity, or tries December, a counter-offer and a larger order |
| **Must understand** | An order is judged on contribution when fixed costs are already covered, and that stops being true when capacity runs out |
| **Decides or infers** | Whether to accept, and the lowest price worth accepting |
| **Feedback** | Change in profit, capacity used and regular sales displaced (shown as a bar), the lowest acceptable price, a reading, and the working including the contribution lost on displaced sales |
| **Misconception diagnosed** | An order priced below average cost must lose money; and its opposite, that any order above variable cost is worth taking |
| **Objective** | Use contribution to decide on a special order, and state the conditions under which the answer changes |

## 5. Ansoff placer

`growth-ansoff-marigold`, on the growth brief and the Ansoff matrix guide.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **The learner does** | Places eight of Marigold's options on the matrix; placements appear in the cells as they choose |
| **Must understand** | The matrix classifies the direction of growth (product and market), not the method (franchising, joint venture, acquisition) |
| **Decides or infers** | What counts as a new product or a new market for each option |
| **Feedback** | Right, Arguable or Not this one, with the reason and any placement that can also be argued |
| **Misconception diagnosed** | Placing a method, such as franchising, as if it were a direction; treating the risk ranking as a rule |
| **Objective** | Classify growth options and argue a placement where it is not clear-cut |

## 6. Decision tree lab

`growth-tree-marigold`, on the growth brief and the decision trees guide.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **The learner does** | Predicts which of three routes has the highest expected value, then sees the tree, the table and the workings, and can change probabilities, payoffs and costs |
| **Must understand** | Expected value is the probability-weighted payoff minus the cost, an average over many repetitions, not a forecast |
| **Decides or infers** | The prediction, then how sensitive the ranking is to the probability of strong demand |
| **Feedback** | Whether the prediction was right, and if the learner chose the route with the highest payoff before cost, why the cost matters; probabilities that do not add up to one are flagged per route; the exact probability at which the ranking changes |
| **Misconception diagnosed** | Forgetting the cost; probabilities that do not add up to one; treating the highest expected value as the decision |
| **Objective** | Calculate expected values and judge how far to trust them |

## 7. Scale explorer

`growth-scale-explorer`, on the growth brief.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **The learner does** | Predicts the chain size with the lowest average cost, then moves through sizes from 1 to 24 restaurants |
| **Must understand** | Average cost falls with purchasing economies and the spreading of central costs, then rises as coordination costs grow faster |
| **Decides or infers** | Where the lowest point is, and which effect dominates at each size |
| **Feedback** | Average cost broken into its parts beside a reference size, a reading naming economies or diseconomies of scale, and feedback on the prediction |
| **Misconception diagnosed** | Bigger is always cheaper; the U shape is something to memorize rather than explain |
| **Objective** | Explain economies and diseconomies of scale from their causes |

## 8. Force field builders (HL)

`growth-forcefield-marigold` and `lead-forcefield-halden`, on the growth and leadership briefs and the force field analysis guide. HL only in both briefs, because force field analysis is reported to be an HL tool.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **The learner does** | Weighs each driving and restraining force from 1 to 5, chooses a restraining force that could be weakened, and writes how |
| **Must understand** | Force field analysis summarizes judgement; its value is the question it leaves, which force to weaken and at what cost |
| **Decides or infers** | The strengths, and the force most worth weakening |
| **Feedback** | Totals and a balance statement as the sliders move; the effect of weakening the chosen force by two points |
| **Misconception diagnosed** | Treating the totals as a measurement |
| **Objective** | Use force field analysis to plan change, and connect it to leadership (most restraining forces at Halden are leadership problems) |

## 11. Decision Rooms

`marigold-growth`, `atelier-pricing`, `halden-leadership`.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **The learner does** | Reads the situation and the aims, commits to a first decision with a written reason, sees its consequence, decides how to implement it, then reads the debrief |
| **Must understand** | Decisions have trade-offs across objectives and stakeholders, and implementation changes the outcome |
| **Decides or infers** | Which aim to put first, which choice serves it, and how to make it work |
| **Feedback** | A board pack of measures (now, after, change, written as Better or Worse), a narrative consequence, how each stakeholder reacts, the risks, the theory, and a debrief comparing every first decision side by side and echoing the learner's own reasoning |
| **Misconception diagnosed** | Per room: growth is always good, or one route is right everywhere; average cost is the benchmark for an order; one leadership style fits every shift |
| **Objective** | Make, justify and evaluate a business decision, the habit Paper 1 rewards |

## 12. Practice question self-check

Every practice question.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **The learner does** | Writes an answer, opens the mark scheme, ticks the points the answer makes or picks the level that fits it |
| **Must understand** | What the command term and the marks ask for |
| **Decides or infers** | Whether the answer actually makes each point |
| **Feedback** | A self-assessed mark, capped at the total, and the errors that cost marks |
| **Misconception diagnosed** | Writing more is the same as writing to the mark scheme |
| **Objective** | Judge an answer against criteria, then improve it |

## 14. Market share and growth workspace

`market-share-porto`, in the Market Studio. Subtopic 4.1, AO2 and AO4; the closing question is HL (AO3).

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **The learner does** | Edits two years of sales for five sellers in a simulated market of hand-made mugs in Porto, or runs a flat market or a price rise to €24 |
| **Must understand** | A share is relative to the whole market: a firm can sell more and still lose share, or sell less and gain it |
| **Decides or infers** | Whether Atelier Barro is gaining ground, and (HL) how much market share matters to a premium studio |
| **Feedback** | Market size and growth, each seller's share, change in percentage points and sales growth, the leader, a reading that names the misleading cases and who overtook whom, a paired bar chart with a table, and the working for the share and for growth; invalid sales are named beside the field |
| **Misconception diagnosed** | Rising sales mean a rising share; a group of small sellers is the market leader; volume share and value share are the same thing |
| **Objective** | Calculate and interpret market share and market growth. Both are standard definitions; the page says that the guide's formula list does not include them |

## 15. Position map builder

`market-position-map-porto`, in the Market Studio. Subtopic 4.2, AO2 and AO4.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **The learner does** | Chooses two of four attributes for the axes of a map built from a simulated survey of 200 buyers, then says which part of the map is empty (or that none is) |
| **Must understand** | A position map shows perceptions from one sample; different attributes give different maps |
| **Decides or infers** | Where the gap is, and whether it is worth filling |
| **Feedback** | The map and its table redraw for every pair of axes; the check marks the choice right or not, shows the answer, shades the empty part of the map and explains whether the gap is an opening or empty for a good reason |

## 16. Capacity utilization and productivity (HL)

`ops-capacity-atelier`, on the Operations Floor. Subtopic 5.6, HL only, AO2 and AO4.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **The learner does** | Moves between October, November with the hotel order and December at Atelier Barro's studio, or enters figures of their own |
| **Must understand** | Utilization spreads fixed costs but removes slack; the productivity rate needs output and input in the same units; rejected units are output that never reaches a customer |
| **Decides or infers** | How hard the kiln is working, and what that means for cost and risk |
| **Feedback** | Capacity utilization, defect rate, labour productivity, productivity rate and the fixed cost carried by each mug, a capacity bar with the busy zone marked, a reading, and the working for each measure; impossible figures (output above capacity, units that do not add up) are named beside the field |
| **Misconception diagnosed** | Higher utilization is always better; productivity rate in mixed units; good output and units made are the same |
| **Objective** | Calculate and interpret the guide's capacity utilization and productivity rate formulas (p. 65), with labour productivity and defect rate as standard definitions |

## 17. Stock control chart (HL)

`ops-stock-control-atelier`, on the Operations Floor. Subtopic 5.6, HL only, AO2 and AO4.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **The learner does** | Sets usage, lead time, buffer stock and reorder quantity for the studio's clay, runs a busy November and a late delivery, and answers three questions |
| **Must understand** | The reorder level covers usage during the lead time on top of the buffer; the buffer is insurance against usage above plan and late deliveries |
| **Decides or infers** | The reorder level, the maximum stock, and how much of the buffer a scenario uses |
| **Feedback** | Reorder level, maximum stock, weeks an order lasts and the lowest stock; a saw-tooth chart (planned line dotted under a scenario) with the lead time, the delivery and the lowest point labelled, and a week-by-week table; each answer is checked against the plan on screen, with targeted feedback for the usual wrong answers |
| **Misconception diagnosed** | The reorder level is the buffer; the reorder level is only the usage during the lead time; maximum stock is the reorder quantity; buffer stock is waste |
| **Objective** | Construct and interpret a stock control chart, and evaluate the size of the buffer |

## 18. Location weighted scoring

`ops-location-marigold`, on the Operations Floor. Subtopic 5.4, AO2.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **The learner does** | Weighs six location factors from 0 to 5 for Marigold Kitchens' first Mumbai restaurant, against scores given for three simulated sites, and writes a recommendation |
| **Must understand** | A weighted score turns judgements into numbers; the answer depends on the weights, and a total can hide a weakness |
| **Decides or infers** | Which factors matter most, and whether any low score rules a site out |
| **Feedback** | Weighted totals and ranking as the weights move; a reading that states the nearest weight for each factor at which the leader changes or a tie appears; the scores of 3 or below flagged; a caution listing the qualitative factors the table leaves out |
| **Misconception diagnosed** | A weighted score is an objective answer; the highest total has no fatal weakness |
| **Objective** | Explain reasons for a location decision, and judge how far a weighted score can be trusted. Weighted scoring is a decision method, not one of the guide's toolkit tools |
